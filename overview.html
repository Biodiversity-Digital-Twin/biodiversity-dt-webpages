Project Overview

Understanding the scope and objectives of the Earth System Digital Twin

Mission Statement

We will design and develop an Earth System Digital Twin (ESDT) to represent and forecast carbon storage dynamics and habitat connectivity across Central Africa. The proposed ESDT will integrate models that quantify and predict the impacts of key stressors on the region's ecosystems, including climate change, land-use change, and biodiversity loss.

This system will provide a digital representation and near-term forecasts of tropical forest structure, biomass, and biodiversity corridors across Central Africa, supporting the scientific objectives of PANGEA, a new terrestrial ecology field campaign.

Study Area

Our project focuses on the Central African region, encompassing the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC), Republic of the Congo, Cameroon, Gabon, and Equatorial Guinea. Three-Phase Approach

What Then

Historical State Analysis

Understanding past conditions and drivers of change through long-term Earth observation records and historical data analysis.

What Now

Current State Characterization

Providing up-to-date assessments of forest structure, carbon storage, and biodiversity corridors using cutting-edge remote sensing and field measurements.

What Next

Future Forecasting

Projecting future ecosystem states under various climate and land-use scenarios to inform conservation strategies and policy decisions.

Key Objectives

  • Integrate spaceborne, airborne, and in situ measurements to characterize forest structure and carbon storage
  • Quantify regional drivers of deforestation and habitat fragmentation
  • Model habitat suitability and biodiversity corridors for key species
  • Forecast changes in forest extent, carbon storage, and ecosystem services
  • Project future ecosystem states under climate change scenarios
  • Provide actionable insights for conservation planning and policy development

Supporting PANGEA

Our Earth System Digital Twin directly supports the PANGEA terrestrial ecology field campaign by:

  • Providing spatial context for field observations and measurements
  • Integrating multiple data streams from satellite, airborne, and ground-based sensors
  • Enabling hypothesis testing through data-model integration
  • Facilitating scenario exploration for conservation planning
  • Delivering near-term forecasts to guide adaptive management strategies
