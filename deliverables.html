Project Deliverables

Datasets and models across three foundational pillars

Deliverables Overview

Our project will deliver nine major datasets and models organized across three pillars: observational foundations, process models, and forecasts. Each deliverable is the responsibility of partner institutions with specialized expertise.

# Deliverables Organization
Pillar 1 - Observational Foundation (historical to present)
1 Wall-to-wall forest structure time-series (2017–present)

Integration of GEDI spaceborne LiDAR, Landsat optical imagery, and Sentinel radar data to produce comprehensive forest structure metrics including canopy height, vertical structure, and biomass estimates.

 Maryland
2 LCLUC time-series (2000–present)

Harmonized legacy land-cover products (MODIS MCD12Q1, ESA CCI, GLAD, Dynamic World) and deforestation products (RADD, GFC, TMF) providing consistent land cover and land use change records.

 JPL
3 LCLUC driver stack

Comprehensive dataset including population density, road networks, hydrology (stream location and depth), urban footprint, night-time lights, protected areas, and topography.

 MIT
Pillar 2 - Process Models (current, explanatory)
4 Driver-LCLUC linkage models

Data-driven or process-based models linking socio-economic and environmental drivers (#3) to observed land cover and land use change (#2).

 MIT
5 Habitat suitability and biodiversity corridors

Models combining forest structure (#1) with animal movement data to identify suitable habitat and critical connectivity corridors for key species.

 NCMNS
Pillar 3 - Forecasts (present–2050; vegetation to 2100)
6 Vegetation demographic model time-series (2017–2100)

ED2.2 ecosystem demography model simulations projecting forest structure, composition, and carbon dynamics under climate change scenarios.

 Maryland and Cornell
7 Driver forecasts (present–2050)

Projected changes in socio-economic and environmental drivers based on current trends and scenario assumptions.

 MIT
8 LCLUC forecasts (present–2050)

Projected land cover and land use change using driver forecasts (#7) and observed historical trends (#2).

 MIT
9 Forecasted habitat suitability and corridors

Future projections of habitat suitability and biodiversity corridors combining habitat models (#5) with vegetation forecasts (#6) and LCLUC projections (#8).

 NCMNS
